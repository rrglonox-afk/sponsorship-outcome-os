How to keep your shirt sponsor

Practical steps for football club commercial teams, based on five seasons of shirt-sponsor data from 128 UK clubs.

Read the researchBook a 15-minute call

For a lower-league or non-league club, the front-of-shirt deal is often the biggest single commercial contract, and it comes up for renewal more often than most people think. In our five-season study of 128 UK clubs, 30% had a different shirt sponsor the following season. This guide sets out what the data says and what to do about it.

What the data says

  • Three in ten change every summer. 105 of 350 season-to-season comparisons, Championship to National League and the Scottish Championship.
  • New sponsors are the likeliest to go. 67% of new shirt sponsors came back for a second season, and 46% reached a third.
  • Changing division hurts. 61% kept their sponsor after promotion or relegation, against 72% when the club stayed put.
  • Deals are short. Announced lengths were one to five seasons, most often two.

Seven ways to keep your shirt sponsor

1. Find out what the sponsor actually wants

Local sponsors buy for different reasons: customers, staff, introductions to other businesses, or standing in the town. Ask at the start and write it down, in their words. At Chesterfield, a construction sponsor left saying the reciprocal business it hoped for never came, and the club director said the concern had never been raised with him (Derbyshire Times).

2. Track at least one thing that ties to sales

A promo code, a tagged link or QR code on the matchday programme, or a log of introductions you make. One tracked number beats any amount of reach. See how to prove ROI to a local business.

3. Hold a mid-season review

One hour with the person who'll make the renewal decision. Ask what's working and what they'll need to see. It's how you hear a concern before it turns into a decision.

4. Start the renewal six months out

With deals running one to three seasons, the next renewal is never far away. Put the date in the diary the day the contract is signed and start the conversation half a year ahead.

5. Agree a division clause

Promotion can make the shirt worth more; relegation can make it worth less to the sponsor. Agree the price and package for each outcome in the contract, so a change of division doesn't reopen the whole deal.

6. Send evidence, not a recap deck

Before the renewal meeting, send a short pack: the sponsor's objective, what the deal delivered and how sure you are, delivery against contract, and a priced offer for next season. Here's exactly what to send.

7. Know your exposure, and have a shortlist

Watch what's happening at the sponsor: a takeover, a new marketing director, cost pressure in their sector. When Pendragon was bought, its BTCC title deal ended two years early (Autosport). If the risk is high, build a replacement shortlist a season early.

When it ends anyway

Some sponsors leave for reasons no club can fix: geography, insolvency, a change of strategy. Gillingham's 12-year sponsor MEMS left in 2023 when the club changed its commercial model, and came back to the front of the shirt in 2025 (Gillingham FC). End well: thank them publicly, keep them informed, and keep the door open.

Questions we get asked

How do I keep my club's shirt sponsor?

Agree what the sponsor wants at the start, track at least one thing that ties to sales, review mid-season, start the renewal six months out, agree a division clause and send an evidence pack before the renewal meeting.

How many shirt sponsors renew?

In our data for five UK leagues, 70% of clubs kept the same front-of-shirt sponsor from one season to the next, and 67% of new sponsors came back for a second season.

Does relegation mean losing the sponsor?

Not always, but it raises the risk: 61% of clubs that changed division kept their sponsor, against 72% that stayed put.
