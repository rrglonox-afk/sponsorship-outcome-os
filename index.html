The Rightsworth standard

Every number is graded.

A finance director's first question is "how do you know?". So every result we report carries a grade for how strong its evidence is. Pick a grade to see what it means.

ROI, done honestly

Never one inflated number. Always a range.

The conservative case counts only what we can track or compare. The base case adds half-credit for what moved without tracking. Media value sits beside ROI as context, and is never counted inside it. Read the full guide to measuring sponsorship ROI →

    FAQ

    Straight answers.

    Anything else? Email raymond@rightsworth.com

    What data do you need from us?

    The exports you already have: the contract and delivery logs, promo-code reports, CRM lead totals, sales or account totals, and any exposure reports. Up to four sources for a single report.

    Do you need any personal or customer-level data?

    No. We work with totals and aggregates only. Nothing personal is shared, and we never train AI models on customer data.

    What if our tracking is weak?

    That's common, and it's exactly what the grades are for. We report what the evidence supports, say how strong it is, and give you a measurement plan so next season can reach grade A or B. If we can't link a single commercial outcome to the deal, you pay half.

    How is this different from exposure measurement?

    Exposure providers measure who saw the logo, and do it well. We use their reports as grade D context, then connect the partnership to the sponsor's leads, codes, sales and customers. The two work together.

    Which sports and properties do you cover?

    Any sport: football, baseball, basketball, motorsport, hockey, rugby, running and more. Clubs, leagues, tracks, venues, events and the brands that sponsor them.

    When should we start?

    Three to six months before the renewal meeting is ideal. That leaves time to gather the data, review the report and build the next-year package into your pitch.
