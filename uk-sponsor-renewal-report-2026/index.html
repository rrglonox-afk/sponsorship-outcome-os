The UK Sponsor Renewal Report 2026

Five seasons of front-of-shirt sponsors at 128 clubs across the Championship, League One, League Two, National League and Scottish Championship: who stayed, who changed, and what was said when deals ended.

Every summer, lower-league clubs across the UK either keep their main sponsor or go looking for a new one. We wanted to know how often each happens, so we recorded the front-of-shirt sponsor of every club in the Championship, League One, League Two, National League and Scottish Championship for five seasons, from 2021-22 to 2025-26, and checked what was said in public when deals ended.

Download the full report (PDF, 8 pages) or the dataset (CSV).

Key findings

  1. Seven in ten stay, three in ten change. In 350 season-to-season comparisons, 245 clubs kept the same front-of-shirt sponsor and 105 changed.
  2. Churn is highest near the top. 65% of Championship clubs kept their sponsor, against 78% in the National League.
  3. Promotion and relegation reopen the deal. Clubs that changed division kept their sponsor 61% of the time, against 72% for clubs that stayed put.
  4. The first renewal is the danger zone. Of 85 new shirt sponsors, 57 (67%) came back for a second season. Of 56 we could follow further, 26 (46%) reached a third.
  5. Deals are short, partnerships are long. Announced deal lengths ran from one to five seasons, most often two. 15 of the 104 sponsors on shirts in 2025-26 had been there all five seasons we tracked.
  6. Exits rarely mention results. In eight public exit stories, the reasons given were strategy, costs, reputation, insolvency, a change of owner and geography. Only one mentioned outcomes, and nobody put a number on them.

Why sponsors leave: what was said in public

Gillingham and MEMS Power Generation (club strategy, 2023)

After 12 years as main sponsor, MEMS's deal ended when the club changed its commercial model. Head of commercial Joe Comper said the club had "relied heavily on one business occupying the majority of our sponsorship inventory". MEMS sponsored Notts County, then returned to the front of Gillingham's shirts for 2025-26. KentOnline, Gillingham FC

Chesterfield and GF Tomlinson (outcomes not met, c. 2019)

The construction firm didn't take up its option to renew, saying the reciprocal business it hoped for never came: "We haven't been able to come to any reciprocal agreements." A club director said the concerns hadn't been raised with him. Derbyshire Times

Preston North End and PAR Group (fan feeling, 2021)

The club moved from betting sponsors to a local manufacturer. Adviser Peter Ridsdale: "We were mindful that some of the supporters had issues with us being sponsored by betting companies." PAR Group stayed on the shirt for four seasons. Chorley Guardian

Raith Rovers and Val McDermid (reputation, 2022)

The author ended her shirt sponsorship after the club's signing of David Goodwillie drew strong criticism, and the away-shirt sponsor also withdrew. The Courier

Bristol City and Huboo (sponsor insolvency, 2025)

The deal ended midway through 2024-25 after the sponsor went into administration. Bristol24/7

Stocksbridge Park Steels and Eco Power (geography)

The sponsor didn't extend into a third year, saying it wanted to "focus our commercial support more locally". Eco-Power Group has sponsored Doncaster Rovers' shirt since 2022-23. The Star

Rugby League and Betfred (cost pressure, 2026)

Betfred is ending nearly ten years as title sponsor after the 2026 season. Founder Fred Done: "you have to look at all your costs." Gambling.com

Power Maxed Racing and Pendragon (change of owner, 2025)

After Pendragon was bought, its BTCC title deal ended two years early. The team called it "unexpected and late". Autosport

The pattern: deals rarely end over a measured result. They end in a cost review, a strategy change, a takeover or a bad headline, when nobody has evidence of what the deal delivered.

What clubs can do with this

  1. Treat the first renewal as the danger zone. Write down what the sponsor wants from the deal at kick-off, in their words.
  2. Start the renewal six months out. Put each renewal date in the diary the day the contract is signed.
  3. Agree a division clause. Price and package for promotion, relegation and staying put.
  4. Bring evidence, not just exposure. Codes, tagged enquiries and introductions logged through the season. Read what to send a sponsor before renewal.
  5. Hear concerns before the press does. A mid-season review costs an hour.

Method and caveats

We took the home front-of-shirt sponsor for each club and season from the "Personnel and kits" tables of the Wikipedia season articles (EFL 2021-22 to 2025-26; National League and Scottish Championship 2022-23 to 2025-26), accessed 14 November 2026, and followed each club across divisions: 496 club-seasons and 357 consecutive-season pairs. We left out 7 pairs with no single named sponsor or where sources disagreed. We checked about 36 club-seasons against club and sponsor announcements and found three differences, which we corrected or excluded.

  • A change isn't a failure. A new name on the shirt can be a better-paying replacement, a charity slot or a sponsor moving to another asset. We measure change, not the reason.
  • Front of shirt only. Sleeve, stadium and other deals aren't counted.
  • Five seasons cuts off longer histories.
  • Football only in the numbers. We found no consistent public season tables for rugby, cricket, basketball or motorsport, so those appear only in the stories.
  • Public statements are partial. We quote them as published and didn't contact any club or sponsor.

Questions we get asked

How often do football clubs change shirt sponsor?

In our data, about three in ten clubs had a different front-of-shirt sponsor the following season: 105 of 350 season-to-season comparisons across five UK leagues, 2021-22 to 2025-26.

How long do lower-league shirt sponsorship deals last?

Where a length was announced, deals ran one to five seasons, most often two. Long partnerships are usually a run of short renewals.
