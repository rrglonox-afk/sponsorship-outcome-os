What to send a sponsor before renewal

A recap deck shows the logo was seen. A renewal pack shows what the deal sold. Here's what to put in it, when to send it, and a template for getting the data.

See a sample reportBook a 15-minute call

The short answer: about three months before the renewal meeting, send the sponsor a short pack with five things in it. What the deal set out to do, what it delivered commercially and how sure you are of each number, what you delivered against the contract, what you'd change next season and at what price, and how you'll measure it next time. Keep exposure figures in, but as context, not as the headline.

It matters because renewals are lost more often than most clubs think. In our UK Sponsor Renewal Report 2026, three in ten front-of-shirt sponsors changed each summer, and a third of new sponsors didn't make a second season.

Why a recap deck isn't enough

Most end-of-season packs are recap decks: photos, impressions, social reach and a media value figure. They show the logo was seen. They don't answer the question the sponsor's finance director will ask: what did this sell? Sponsors rarely leave because a deal was measured and failed. They leave in a cost review, after a takeover or when a new marketing director arrives, and a recap deck gives that person nothing to defend.

The five-part renewal pack

1. A one-page summary

Write it for someone who wasn't there when the deal was signed. What the sponsor wanted (in their words), the three strongest results, anything that didn't work, and your recommendation for next season. If they read nothing else, this page should carry the renewal.

2. Commercial outcomes, graded by evidence

List what the partnership produced for the sponsor's business: promo-code orders, tagged leads, enquiries, new customers, introductions that turned into work. Give each one a grade so the sponsor can see how solid it is:

GradeMeansExample
ACompared against no sponsorshipNew customers in your area against an area you don't reach
BTracked to the dealPromo-code redemptions, tagged CRM leads
CMoved during the deal, not trackedEnquiries up during the season, no tag
DSeen, not soldImpressions, reach, media value

Grading is what makes the pack credible. A sponsor will trust a modest, well-evidenced result over a large unexplained one. The full method is in our guide to measuring sponsorship ROI.

3. Delivery against the contract

Every asset in the contract, what was delivered and any gaps: LED minutes, programme pages, hospitality used, social posts, player appearances. If something was missed, say so and say what you'll do about it. Finding a gap yourself builds more trust than having the sponsor find it.

4. Next season: what to keep, cut and add, with a price

Use the evidence to shape the offer. Drop what didn't work, put more into what did, and price it. Include what happens if you're promoted or relegated: changing division is one of the moments a renewal is most at risk.

5. A measurement plan

Show how next season will produce better evidence: a code per channel, a tagged link or QR code, a source field in the sponsor's CRM, an introductions log and an agreed value for a lead or customer. It tells the sponsor you're serious about results, and it makes next year's renewal easier.

When to send it

WhenWhat
Contract signedAgree the sponsor's objective in writing. Set up codes, links and an introductions log.
Mid-seasonOne-hour review with the decision-maker.
6 months outAsk the sponsor for their side of the data (template below).
3 months outSend the renewal pack.
Renewal meetingWalk through it and agree next season.

What not to send

  • Media value presented as ROI. It's the first number a finance team throws out. Keep it as context.
  • A 40-slide deck. Put the detail in an appendix. The decision-maker reads page one.
  • Only good news. A pack with no weaknesses reads as marketing. Name what didn't work and the fix.
  • Numbers with no source. Every figure should say where it came from.

If you didn't track anything this season

You can still send a useful pack. Pull the promo-code report if there was one, your delivery logs and any hospitality guest lists. Ask the sponsor three questions: did customers mention the club, did enquiries change during the season, and did any introductions lead to work? Grade what you get honestly, then make the measurement plan the centrepiece of the renewal.

Template: asking the sponsor for data

Subject: Your [season] partnership review

Hi [name], we're putting together a review of this season's partnership ahead of our conversation in [month]. To show what it's done for [sponsor], could you share any of these? Totals are fine, nothing personal:

  • orders or sign-ups using the code [CODE]
  • enquiries or leads tagged to the club, or that mentioned us
  • any work that came from introductions through the club
  • roughly what a new customer is worth to you

Anything you can't share is fine. We'll grade what we have and show you what's solid. Thanks, [name]

Questions we get asked

What should be in a sponsorship recap report?

A summary page, commercial outcomes graded by evidence, delivery against the contract, a priced plan for next season and a measurement plan. Exposure figures belong in it as context.

When should I send a sponsor the renewal pack?

About three months before the renewal meeting, after asking the sponsor for their side of the data around six months out.

What if the sponsor won't share sales data?

Use what you control: promo-code reports, tagged links, delivery logs and an introductions log. Grade it honestly and agree better tracking for next season.
